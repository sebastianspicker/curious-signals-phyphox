## Physical boundaries and estimator choices

- **Pressure** is now explicitly hydrostatic relative-height inference with bracketed drift references. Removed its extended water-boiling/Clapeyron worked section. Retained the sealed-bag excursion only as a causal counterexample to converting every pressure change into height. A new traverse schematic explains the moving instrument and return to the reference height. The estimator uses actual mean timestamps, interpolation weights and covariance of before/event/after means. Clarified that a stable additive offset cancels exactly from a pressure difference but only approximately from its logarithmic height reduction; a common multiplicative scale cancels from a pressure ratio. Removed the inherited claim that the example timestamp proves a particular physical conversion instant; cadence alone does not establish timing or bandwidth.
- **Pressure-dependent boiling** retains the five code1 observations, saturation residuals and apparent Clausius--Clapeyron enthalpy. It is the canonical home for the concise Martian pressure/triple-point/instrument-domain comparison previously duplicated in `pressure` and `mission-to-mars`. The LPS22HB/BMP280 lower limits both exclude observation IDs11–15, including two sustained-boiling states. These limits are not assigned to the actual independent wet-compatible instrument of the manual observation method. No solid-phase or planetary-environment measurement is inferred.
- **Spectral fingerprints** retains the common-wavelength sample/star/continuum/dark ratios and the useful local feature comparison. Removed a rhetorical claim about an imagined “55% line” in favour of directly explaining why the reference-relative depression cannot all be attributed to the additional absorber. Clarified that it is uncertainty in a shared dark estimate that correlates ratios. The 15-nm grid and unspecified LSF remain separate from the 0.2-nm wavelength calibration uncertainty.
- **Coronagraph contrast** retains all six separations and both mask states, including the result that a lower background does not improve the reported detection statistic. The general ROI-mean covariance equation now precedes the independent-within-ROI pixel approximation, making the two independence assumptions distinct. Table subscripts0/1 are explicitly defined as absent/present mask. The shorter title stresses background suppression and detectability without equating ROI retention to calibrated target throughput.
